How to Reactivate a Deactivated SIM Card

In India, telecom operators are legally obligated by the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) to recycle inactive mobile numbers. Because the pool of 10-digit mobile numbers is finite, operators cannot afford to leave millions of unused numbers sitting in a drawer forever. If you have neglected a secondary SIM card and it suddenly shows "No Service," you are likely dealing with a deactivated number.

However, "deactivated" can mean two things: temporarily suspended (which is easily fixable) or permanently recycled. In this guide, we break down the strict timeline operators follow and how you can save your number.

The Deactivation Timeline (The 90-Day Rule)

TRAI mandates a specific grace period before an operator can snatch your number away. Here is how the timeline works from the exact day your current recharge plan expires:

  • Day 1 to 7 (Grace Period 1): Your outgoing calls and data are blocked. However, you will still receive incoming calls and SMS.
  • Day 8 to 90 (Grace Period 2): Incoming calls and SMS are completely blocked. Anyone calling you will hear "The number you are calling is currently out of service." Your SIM card will eventually lose its network signal entirely.
  • Day 91+: The operator initiates the permanent deactivation process. The number is scrubbed from your name and pushed back into the national numbering pool to be sold to a new customer.

Scenario A: Your SIM Expired Less Than 90 Days Ago

If your SIM card shows no signal, but it has been less than 90 days since your last recharge expired, you are in luck. Your number is simply in a deep suspension state.

  1. Connect your phone to Wi-Fi.
  2. Open a payment app (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm) or the operator's official app.
  3. Enter your mobile number and attempt to do a standard "Unlimited" recharge (like a ₹239 or ₹299 plan). Do NOT buy a small ₹10 talktime top-up; it will not work.
  4. Once the transaction is successful, restart your phone. Within 15 minutes, your network signal will return, and all incoming/outgoing services will be restored.

Scenario B: Your SIM Expired Over 90 Days Ago

If you cross the 90-day threshold, online recharges will fail. The system will throw an error saying "Invalid Number" or "Number does not belong to this operator." This means the number has been officially recycled.

The "15-Day Safe Period" Hack: After permanent deactivation on Day 90, operators usually keep the number in a "quarantine" pool for about 15 days before releasing it to the public. If you act immediately during this tiny window, you can sometimes get it back.

Steps to attempt recovery:

  1. Locate your original Aadhaar card and the physical dead SIM card.
  2. Visit the main company-owned gallery of your operator (Airtel Store, Jio Center, or Vi Store). Do not go to a small corner shop; they do not have the authority for this.
  3. Explain that your number was deactivated due to non-recharge and request a Reactivation / Re-issuance.
  4. The executive will check the database. If the number is still in quarantine and hasn't been bought by a new customer, they will charge you a penalty/re-issuance fee (around ₹50 to ₹100), process your eKYC again, and hand you a fresh SIM card with your old number.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What if someone else has already bought my number?

If the executive checks the system and sees the number is active under a different Aadhaar card, it is permanently gone. You have zero legal rights to claim the number back, even if it is linked to your bank accounts. You must immediately contact your bank to update your records.

Will I get my old main account balance back upon reactivation?

If you reactivated the SIM within the 90-day grace period by doing a recharge, any core talktime balance you had previously is usually restored. However, if the number crossed 90 days and had to be completely re-issued with new KYC, your old balance is forfeited.
